Module 1 Summary

Review these key points from all three lessons before taking the Module 1 Graded Quiz. The graded quiz covers material from all three lessons.

1
Lesson 1: Nature, Meaning and Philosophy of Evidence Law
  • Evidence is any material (oral, documentary, or electronic) that a court uses to determine disputed facts
  • Section 2 of the Evidence Act Cap. 8 defines evidence to include witness statements and documents including electronic records
  • Courts cannot guess, assume, or act on personal knowledge - every finding must be based on properly presented material
  • Evidence serves three core purposes: establishing facts, protecting against wrongful decisions, and ensuring fairness
  • A fact is what happened in reality; evidence is the material used to prove that fact in court
  • Evidence law is built on five objectives: truth, fairness, reliability, protection against wrongful conviction, and efficiency
  • Article 28 of the Constitution of Uganda 1995 guarantees the right to a fair hearing
  • Evidence law protects rights, ensures equal treatment, supports sound judicial decision-making, and builds public confidence in courts
2
Lesson 2: Legal Classification of Law
  • Law is divided into two categories: substantive law (creates rights and offences) and adjectival/procedural law (governs how those rights are enforced)
  • Evidence law is procedural (adjectival) law - it governs the process of proof, not the substance of rights
  • Substantive law asks: is this conduct illegal? Evidence law asks: has it been proved that this conduct occurred?
  • The Evidence Act Cap. 8 applies universally across criminal, civil, land, family, and commercial cases
  • A competent advocate must think simultaneously about what to prove (substantive law) and how to prove it (evidence law)
  • Errors in applying evidence law are grounds of appeal, just like errors in applying substantive law
3
Lesson 3: Historical Development of Evidence Law
  • Uganda's evidence law traces its roots to English common law developed through court decisions from the 17th to 19th centuries
  • The Indian Evidence Act 1872, drafted by Sir James Fitzjames Stephen, codified English common law into a clear written statute
  • The Indian Evidence Act 1872 was adopted across British East Africa and became the foundation of Uganda's Evidence Act Cap. 8
  • English and Indian court decisions can be persuasive but not binding authority in Ugandan courts
  • The Constitution of Uganda 1995 (Articles 28, 44, and 26) has added a constitutional dimension to Ugandan evidence law
  • Electronic evidence, confessions, and expert evidence are areas of active development in Ugandan evidence law today

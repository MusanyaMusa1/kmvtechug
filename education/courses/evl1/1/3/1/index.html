Origins of Evidence Law in England

Text~10 min reading

Why History Matters for Ugandan Law Students

Uganda's evidence law did not emerge from nowhere. It has a clear historical origin that explains why the Evidence Act Cap. 8 looks the way it does and why Ugandan courts sometimes refer to English and Indian cases when interpreting the Act. Understanding this history makes you a better lawyer because it helps you understand the purpose behind the rules you are applying.

The English Common Law Foundation

Evidence law as a formal body of rules developed primarily in England between the 17th and 19th centuries. Before this period, English courts had no systematic rules about evidence. Judges admitted whatever they found helpful and excluded whatever they did not. The results were often arbitrary and unjust.

Over time, through the decisions of English courts in actual cases, rules began to emerge. These rules covered who could be a competent witness, when hearsay evidence should be excluded, how confessions should be treated, and what standard of proof was required before a person could be convicted of a crime. This body of rules is what lawyers call the common law - law made by judges through their decisions rather than by Parliament through legislation.

The Problem of Codification

By the 19th century, English evidence law was a complex, sometimes contradictory collection of rules scattered across hundreds of court decisions. It was difficult to know what the law actually was on any given point. This complexity was particularly problematic for administering justice in Britain's colonial territories. How could colonial courts apply a body of law that existed only in centuries of English court decisions?

The Move Towards a Written Code

The solution came through codification - the process of taking rules that existed in case law and rewriting them as a clear, accessible statute. The goal was a single document that stated the rules of evidence in plain, organized form so that any trained lawyer or judge could apply them consistently. This codification effort led directly to the Indian Evidence Act of 1872.

Key Points - Page 1.3.1
  • Uganda's evidence law has its roots in English common law developed between the 17th and 19th centuries
  • English evidence law grew from individual court decisions over centuries, not from any single statute
  • By the 19th century, this body of case law was complex and difficult to apply consistently
  • The solution was codification: turning the rules from case law into a clear written statute
  • This codification process produced the Indian Evidence Act of 1872

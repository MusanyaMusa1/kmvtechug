Section A - Multiple Choice (10 questions)
Question 1 of 20
Which section of the Evidence Act Cap. 8 is the foundational provision establishing what evidence may be given in court?
A
Section 2
B
Section 5
C
Section 30
D
Section 15
Section 5 is the foundational provision. It provides that evidence may be given of facts in issue and of such other facts as are declared relevant by the Act.
Not correct. Section 5 is the foundational relevancy provision. The correct answer is B.
Question 2 of 20
A prosecution witness in a murder trial in Kampala testifies that one week before the killing, he saw the accused buying a large hunting knife from a market in Owino. Is this evidence relevant?
A
No - buying a knife is not a crime and therefore irrelevant
B
Yes - it may show preparation under Section 8 making it more probable the accused had a weapon
C
No - only the actual weapon found at the scene is relevant
D
Yes - but only if the witness is a police officer
The purchase of a knife one week before a murder is relevant under Section 8 as evidence of preparation. It makes it more probable the accused had access to a weapon. Relevancy does not require that buying the knife was itself a crime.
Not correct. This is relevant under Section 8 as evidence of preparation. The correct answer is B.
Question 3 of 20
In a contract dispute, a business partner argues money was transferred as a loan not a gift. The claimant produces bank transfer records and text messages about repayment. What type of facts are the bank records and text messages?
A
Facts in issue
B
Irrelevant facts
C
Relevant facts
D
Privileged facts
The bank records and text messages are relevant facts. They are not themselves the facts in issue but have a logical connection to the question of whether the money was a loan, making that more probable.
Not correct. These are relevant facts - they make the facts in issue more or less probable. The correct answer is C.
Question 4 of 20
Makin v Attorney General of New South Wales [1894] AC 57 is authority for which principle in Ugandan evidence law?
A
The rule against hearsay
B
The standard of proof in criminal cases
C
Similar fact evidence may be admissible to show a system or pattern connected to the matter in issue
D
Confessions must be made before a magistrate to be admissible
Makin v AG of NSW is the foundational Commonwealth case on similar fact evidence. The Privy Council held evidence of similar acts may be admissible where relevant to an issue - such as showing a system or plan - but not merely to show the accused is a bad person. It is persuasive authority for Section 15.
Not correct. Makin v AG of NSW is authority on similar fact evidence under Section 15. The correct answer is C.
Question 5 of 20
Which of the following is an example of evidence that is relevant but inadmissible in a Ugandan court?
A
A properly authenticated sale agreement produced in a contract dispute
B
A confession obtained through torture from an accused person
C
Eyewitness testimony from a competent adult who personally observed the offence
D
A certified copy of a public document produced in accordance with the Evidence Act Cap. 8
A confession obtained through torture is relevant - it addresses whether the accused committed the offence. However, it is inadmissible because it was obtained in violation of Article 24 of the Constitution. This is the clearest example of evidence that is relevant but inadmissible.
Not correct. A torture-obtained confession is relevant but inadmissible under Article 24 of the Constitution. The correct answer is B.
Question 6 of 20
What does the res gestae doctrine under Section 6 of the Evidence Act Cap. 8 declare relevant?
A
Facts that occurred many years before the fact in issue
B
Facts that form part of the same transaction as a fact in issue
C
Facts about the general character of witnesses
D
Facts discovered after the court proceedings have concluded
Section 6 declares relevant facts that form part of the same transaction as a fact in issue. This is the res gestae doctrine. Words shouted during a robbery, reactions of bystanders, and the immediate pursuit of the perpetrator all form part of the same transaction.
Not correct. Section 6 (res gestae) declares facts forming part of the same transaction relevant. The correct answer is B.
Question 7 of 20
In a Ugandan court, which question is addressed first - relevancy or admissibility?
A
Admissibility - because it is the more important question
B
Neither - they are addressed simultaneously
C
Relevancy - because evidence that is not relevant cannot be admitted and admissibility never arises
D
Admissibility - because if evidence is admissible it must also be relevant
Relevancy is always addressed first. Evidence that is not relevant cannot be admitted regardless of any other consideration. Only if evidence passes the relevancy test does the court then consider whether it is admissible.
Not correct. Relevancy is always addressed first. If evidence fails the relevancy test, admissibility never arises. The correct answer is C.
Question 8 of 20

Stanbic Bank Uganda Ltd v Uganda Crocs Ltd confirmed that documents must be properly authenticated before they can be admitted. Relevancy alone is not sufficient - the specific admissibility requirements for documentary evidence must also be satisfied.
Not correct. Stanbic Bank v Uganda Crocs confirmed that documents must be properly authenticated before admission. The correct answer is B.
Question 9 of 20

B
Article 50 - enforcement of rights

Article 24 - prohibition of torture, cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment

Article 24 of the Constitution of Uganda 1995 directly prohibits torture, cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment or punishment. This is the constitutional basis for excluding torture-obtained confessions.

Question 10 of 20
An advocate wants to challenge a prosecution witness whose account has changed significantly from an earlier statement. What is the correct approach?
A
Object on relevancy grounds - the evidence has no logical connection to the case
B
Object on admissibility grounds - inconsistent statements must be excluded
C
Neither relevancy nor admissibility - challenge the weight through cross-examination
D
Apply to the court to have the witness declared hostile
Inconsistencies do not make evidence irrelevant or inadmissible. The correct strategy is to challenge the weight through cross-examination, highlighting the inconsistencies. This is an important practical distinction between admissibility and weight.
Not correct. Inconsistencies do not make evidence inadmissible. Challenge the weight through cross-examination. The correct answer is C.
Section B - True or False (5 questions)
Question 11 of 20
Evidence that is not relevant may still be admitted by a Ugandan court if the judge personally finds it helpful.
False. Evidence that is not relevant cannot be admitted at all regardless of the judge's personal view. Relevancy is the minimum threshold - without it, evidence cannot enter the court.
False is correct. Evidence that is not relevant cannot be admitted at all. The judge has no discretion to admit irrelevant evidence.
Question 12 of 20
Under Section 30(a) of the Evidence Act Cap. 8, a dying declaration in Uganda requires the declarant to be under a settled expectation of death at the time of making the statement.
False. This is a critical Uganda-specific point. Unlike the old English common law position, Section 30(a) does NOT require the declarant to be under a settled expectation of death. A dying declaration is admissible in Uganda if the declarant has since died and the statement relates to the cause of their death.
False is correct. Section 30(a) does NOT require a settled expectation of death in Uganda - this differs from old English common law.
Question 13 of 20
The Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984 (PACE) applies in Uganda and gives Ugandan courts discretion to exclude admissible evidence.
False. PACE 1984 is English legislation and does not apply in Uganda. Uganda's courts derive their power to exclude evidence from the Constitution of Uganda 1995 and the Evidence Act Cap. 8.
False is correct. PACE 1984 is English legislation and does not apply in Uganda.
Question 14 of 20
Admissibility and weight are two different things - evidence may be admissible but carry very little weight in a particular case.
True. Admissibility determines whether evidence gets before the court. Weight determines how much significance the court gives to admitted evidence. A piece of evidence may pass all admissibility requirements but still be given very little weight.
True is correct. Admissibility and weight are two distinct concepts.
Question 15 of 20
Uganda v Doyi Wabwire Kyoyo (1976) HCB 212 established procedural safeguards for magistrates taking confessions in Uganda.
True. Uganda v Doyi Wabwire Kyoyo (1976) HCB 212 is a foundational Ugandan case in which Justice Ssekandi established the step-by-step procedural safeguards that magistrates must follow when taking confessions.
True is correct. Uganda v Doyi Wabwire Kyoyo established the foundational confession safeguards.

Section D - Fill in the Blank (3 questions)
Question 18 of 20 - Fill in the Blank
The classic statement of the relationship between relevancy and admissibility is: all _______ evidence is relevant, but not all relevant evidence is _______. (Both blanks take the same word.)
Type the single word that fills both blanks.
All admissible evidence is relevant - admissibility requires relevancy. But not all relevant evidence is admissible - relevancy is necessary but not sufficient for admissibility.
The answer is 'admissible'. All admissible evidence is relevant, but not all relevant evidence is admissible.
Question 19 of 20 - Fill in the Blank
Under Section _______ of the Evidence Act Cap. 8, facts forming part of the same transaction as a fact in issue are declared relevant. This is sometimes called the res _______ doctrine.
Type the section number only (the second blank is 'gestae' which is part of 'res gestae').
Section 6 declares relevant facts that form part of the same transaction as a fact in issue. This is the res gestae doctrine.
The answer is '6'. Section 6 is the res gestae provision.
Question 20 of 20 - Fill in the Blank
Evidence that passes the relevancy test but is excluded because it was obtained through torture fails at the _______ gate. The constitutional provision that prohibits torture in Uganda is Article _______ of the Constitution of Uganda 1995. (Type either answer - they will be checked separately.)
Type one of the two answers: either 'admissibility' or '24'.
Torture-obtained evidence fails at the admissibility gate - it is relevant but legally excluded. The constitutional basis is Article 24 of the Constitution of Uganda 1995.
The answers are 'admissibility' and '24'. Torture-obtained evidence is relevant but fails the admissibility gate under Article 24.

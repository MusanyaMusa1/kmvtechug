Question 1 of 6
What is the foundational provision on relevancy in the Evidence Act Cap. 8?
A
Section 2, which defines evidence
B
Section 5, which provides that evidence may be given of facts in issue and facts declared relevant
C
Section 30, which deals with hearsay exceptions
D
Section 14, which deals with state of mind
Section 5 of the Evidence Act Cap. 8 is the foundational relevancy provision. It establishes that evidence may be given of facts in issue and of such other facts as are declared relevant by the Act.
Not correct. Section 5 is the foundational relevancy provision - it establishes that evidence may be given of facts in issue and of such other facts as are declared relevant by the Act. The correct answer is B.
Question 2 of 6
A woman is charged with theft. The prosecution wants to introduce evidence that she had debts she could not repay at the time of the alleged theft. Is this evidence relevant?
A
No - her financial situation has nothing to do with the theft
B
Yes - financial pressure may show motive under Section 8 of the Evidence Act Cap. 8
C
No - motive is never relevant in criminal cases
D
Yes - but only if the debt was owed to the same person she stole from
Evidence of financial pressure is relevant under Section 8 as showing motive. Motive is not an element of theft that must be proved, but it is a relevant fact because it makes it more probable that the accused had a reason to commit the act.
Not correct. Evidence of financial pressure is relevant under Section 8 as showing motive. The correct answer is B.
Question 3 of 6
What is the difference between facts in issue and relevant facts?
A
Facts in issue are proved by documents only. Relevant facts are proved by witnesses only.
B
Facts in issue are what must be proved to win the case. Relevant facts have a logical connection to facts in issue and make them more or less probable.
C
There is no difference - they mean the same thing
D
Facts in issue arise from evidence law. Relevant facts arise from substantive law.
Facts in issue arise from the elements of the offence or cause of action as defined by substantive law. Relevant facts are not themselves in issue but have a logical connection to facts in issue, making them more or less probable.
Not correct. Facts in issue arise from substantive law and are what must be proved. Relevant facts are not in issue but connect logically to what is. The correct answer is B.
Question 4 of 6
Under which section of the Evidence Act Cap. 8 would words shouted by an accused during a robbery be classified as relevant?
A
Section 5 - facts in issue
B
Section 8 - motive and preparation
C
Section 6 - facts forming part of the same transaction
D
Section 14 - state of mind
Words shouted during a robbery form part of the same transaction as the robbery itself. Section 6 declares facts forming part of the same transaction as a fact in issue to be relevant. This is the res gestae doctrine.
Not correct. Words shouted during a robbery form part of the same transaction - Section 6 (res gestae). The correct answer is C.
Question 5 of 6
The Privy Council case of Makin v Attorney General of New South Wales [1894] AC 57 is relevant to which section of the Evidence Act Cap. 8?
A
Section 6 - same transaction
B
Section 7 - occasion, cause, and effect
C
Section 15 - similar fact evidence
D
Section 9 - explanatory facts
Makin v AG of New South Wales [1894] AC 57 is the leading Commonwealth case on similar fact evidence. It is persuasive authority for the application of Section 15 of the Evidence Act Cap. 8 which deals with whether an act was accidental or intentional.
Not correct. Makin is the leading case on similar fact evidence - persuasive authority for Section 15. The correct answer is C.
Question 6 of 6
Which of the following correctly states the test for relevancy under the Evidence Act Cap. 8?
A
A fact is relevant if it is interesting or helpful to the court in any way
B
A fact is relevant if the judge personally finds it convincing
C
A fact is relevant if it makes a fact in issue more or less probable
D
A fact is relevant only if it directly proves a fact in issue without any inference required
The test for relevancy is the probative value test - a fact is relevant if it makes a fact in issue more or less probable. Circumstantial evidence requiring an inference can still be highly relevant. The judge's personal view is not the test.
Not correct. The probative value test: a fact is relevant if it makes a fact in issue more or less probable. The correct answer is C.

Question 1 of 6
Which of the following correctly states the relationship between relevancy and admissibility?
A
All relevant evidence is automatically admissible
B
All admissible evidence is relevant, but not all relevant evidence is admissible
C
Admissibility and relevancy mean exactly the same thing
D
Irrelevant evidence can still be admitted if it is very persuasive
All admissible evidence is relevant, but not all relevant evidence is admissible. Relevancy is necessary but not sufficient for admissibility. Admissibility requires relevancy, but relevancy does not guarantee admissibility.
Not correct. The classic statement is: all admissible evidence is relevant, but not all relevant evidence is admissible. The correct answer is B.
Question 2 of 6
A confession is obtained from an accused person through torture at a police station in Kampala. The confession is relevant. Should the court admit it?
A
Yes - it is relevant and directly proves the accused committed the offence
B
Yes - as long as the accused signed it voluntarily after the torture
C
No - a confession obtained through torture violates Article 24 of the Constitution of Uganda 1995 and is inadmissible
D
No - confessions are never admissible in Ugandan courts
Article 24 of the Constitution of Uganda 1995 prohibits torture absolutely. A confession obtained through torture is inadmissible regardless of its relevancy. Confessions are admissible in Uganda but only when voluntarily made in accordance with the Evidence Act Cap. 8.
Not correct. Article 24 prohibits torture absolutely. The confession is inadmissible despite being relevant. The correct answer is C.
Question 3 of 6
What is the difference between the admissibility and the weight of evidence?
A
There is no difference - they are the same thing
B
Admissibility determines whether evidence gets before the court. Weight determines how much significance the court gives to admitted evidence.
C
Weight is decided before admissibility in all Ugandan courts
D
Admissibility applies only to documents. Weight applies only to witness testimony.
Admissibility is the gateway - can this evidence be received by the court? Weight is evaluated after admission - how much significance should the court give it? Confusing the two leads to wrong strategic decisions in court.
Not correct. Admissibility determines whether evidence gets before the court. Weight determines how much significance the court gives to admitted evidence. The correct answer is B.
Question 4 of 6
Which Ugandan case established foundational procedural safeguards for magistrates taking confessions from accused persons?
A
Stanbic Bank Uganda Ltd v Uganda Crocs Ltd, Civil Appeal No. 4 of 2004
B
Akbar Hussein Godi v Uganda, Criminal Appeal No. 3 of 2013
C
Francis Xavier Kayemba v Uganda [1983] HCB 330
D
Uganda v Doyi Wabwire Kyoyo (1976) HCB 212
Uganda v Doyi Wabwire Kyoyo (1976) HCB 212 is the foundational Ugandan case on confession procedures. Justice Ssekandi established the step-by-step procedural safeguards that magistrates must follow when taking confessions to ensure voluntariness and protect against coercion.
Not correct. Uganda v Doyi Wabwire Kyoyo (1976) HCB 212 established the foundational procedural safeguards for magistrates taking confessions. The correct answer is D.
Question 5 of 6
A dying declaration is made by a stabbing victim in Mulago Hospital who names their attacker before dying. Under which provision of the Evidence Act Cap. 8 may this statement be admissible?
A
Section 5 - facts in issue
B
Section 8 - motive and preparation
C
Section 30(a) - dying declarations as an exception to the hearsay rule
D
Section 14 - state of mind
Dying declarations fall within Section 30(a) of the Evidence Act Cap. 8 as a recognised exception to the hearsay rule. An important Uganda-specific point: unlike the old English common law position, the declarant does NOT need to be under a settled expectation of death at the time of making the statement.
Not correct. Dying declarations are admissible under Section 30(a) as an exception to the hearsay rule. The correct answer is C.
Question 6 of 6
The English Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984 gives courts discretion to exclude admissible evidence if its admission would adversely affect the fairness of proceedings. Does this discretion apply in Ugandan courts?
A
Yes - Uganda follows all English evidence law through the common law
B
Yes - but only in the High Court, not magistrates courts
C
No - the Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984 is English legislation and does not apply in Uganda. Uganda's courts derive their exclusionary power from the Constitution of Uganda 1995 and the Evidence Act Cap. 8.
D
No - Ugandan courts have no power to exclude admissible evidence under any circumstances
PACE 1984 is English legislation and does not apply in Uganda. However, Ugandan courts have a constitutionally rooted power to exclude evidence obtained in violation of constitutional rights under Articles 24, 27, and 50 of the Constitution of Uganda 1995.
Not correct. PACE 1984 is English legislation and does not apply in Uganda. The correct answer is C.

Module 5 Summary

Review these key points from both lessons before taking the Module 5 Graded Quiz. The quiz covers all content from Lessons 1 and 2.

1
Lesson 1: The Burden of Proof
  • The burden of proof is the obligation on a party to prove the facts necessary to establish their case
  • Section 101 Evidence Act Cap. 8: whoever asserts a fact must prove it
  • Section 102 provides the practical test: the burden lies on the person who would fail if no evidence at all were given on either side
  • Two types: the legal burden (fixed, does not shift, discharged by end of case) and the evidential burden (can shift between parties during proceedings)
  • In criminal cases the legal burden lies on the prosecution throughout - constitutional requirement under Article 28(3)(a) of the Constitution of Uganda 1995
  • Woolmington v DPP [1935] AC 462: the golden thread principle - it is the duty of the prosecution to prove the prisoner's guilt
  • Francis Xavier Kayemba v Uganda [1983] HCB 330: prima facie case is evidence on which a reasonable tribunal could convict if the defence offers no explanation
  • Section 106: facts especially within a person's knowledge - that person bears the burden
  • Defence of insanity under Section 11 of the Trial on Indictments Act Cap. 25 places a legal burden on the accused on the balance of probabilities
2
Lesson 2: Shifting the Burden
  • When lawyers say the burden shifts in a criminal trial, they mean the evidential burden - not the legal burden
  • The evidential burden works like a spotlight moving between parties as each responds to the other
  • Prosecution bears initial evidential burden - must establish prima facie case before accused is called to answer
  • A no-case submission challenges whether the prosecution has discharged its initial evidential burden
  • When the accused raises a specific defence, the accused bears the evidential burden of raising sufficient evidence
  • The legal burden of disproving any defence never shifts from the prosecution in criminal cases
  • Akbar Hussein Godi v Uganda, Criminal Appeal No. 3 of 2013: suspicion however strong is not proof
  • Civil standard of proof: balance of probabilities - more probable than not
  • Criminal standard of proof: beyond reasonable doubt - the court must be sure
  • In civil cases, burden analysis is done issue by issue - both parties may bear burdens simultaneously on different issues

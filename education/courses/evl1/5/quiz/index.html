Question 2 of 20
Using the Section 102 test, who bears the burden of proof at the start of a criminal trial for theft?
A
The accused - because they must prove innocence
B
The magistrate - because the court must determine the truth
C
The prosecution - because the accused would succeed if no evidence were given on either side
D
Both parties equally
Using the Section 102 test: if no evidence at all were given, the accused would succeed - the court cannot convict without evidence. Therefore the prosecution bears the burden from the outset.
Not correct. Using Section 102: if no evidence were given, the accused would succeed - so the prosecution bears the burden. The correct answer is C.

Question 4 of 20

Question 6 of 20

On self-defence: the accused bears the evidential burden of raising sufficient evidence to make it a live issue. The prosecution must then disprove self-defence beyond reasonable doubt as part of its overall legal burden.

Section B - True or False (5 questions)
Question 11 of 20
The legal burden in a Ugandan criminal trial shifts to the accused once the prosecution establishes a prima facie case.
False. The legal burden in criminal cases never shifts to the accused. What shifts when a prima facie case is established is the evidential burden - the accused must now decide how to respond. The prosecution's legal burden remains.
False is correct. The legal burden never shifts to the accused in a criminal trial.
Question 12 of 20
The defence of insanity in Uganda places a legal burden on the accused to prove insanity on the balance of probabilities.
True. The defence of insanity is a recognised exception to the general rule. Under Section 11 of the Trial on Indictments Act Cap. 25, the burden of proving insanity lies on the accused on the balance of probabilities.
True is correct. The defence of insanity places a legal burden on the accused under Section 11 of the Trial on Indictments Act Cap. 25.
Question 13 of 20
In a civil dispute, both the plaintiff and the defendant can simultaneously bear burdens of proof on different issues within the same proceedings.
True. In civil proceedings, burden analysis is done issue by issue. The plaintiff bears the burden on the elements of the cause of action. The defendant bears the burden on affirmative defences. Both parties may therefore bear burdens simultaneously on different issues.
True is correct. In civil proceedings both parties may bear burdens on different issues simultaneously.
Question 14 of 20
Under Woolmington v DPP [1935] AC 462, the prosecution can satisfy its burden by proving the accused is the most likely suspect.
False. Woolmington v DPP and Akbar Hussein Godi v Uganda make clear that suspicion - even strong suspicion - does not discharge the prosecution's burden. The prosecution must prove guilt beyond reasonable doubt.
False is correct. Suspicion is not proof. Woolmington requires proof of guilt beyond reasonable doubt, not merely that the accused is the most likely suspect.
Question 15 of 20
A no-case submission at the close of the prosecution case challenges whether the prosecution has discharged its initial evidential burden.
True. A no-case submission is exactly a challenge to whether the prosecution has discharged its initial evidential burden - whether it has established a prima facie case using the Francis Xavier Kayemba standard.
True is correct. A no-case submission challenges whether the prosecution has established a prima facie case.

Question 20 of 20 - Fill in the Blank

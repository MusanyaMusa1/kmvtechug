Burden of Proof in Civil Cases

Text~8 min readingFinal page of Lesson 2

The General Rule in Civil Proceedings

In civil proceedings in Uganda, the burden of proof is governed by Section 101 of the Evidence Act Cap. 8 - whoever desires the court to give judgment on the existence of facts they assert must prove those facts. The general principle is that the burden lies on the party who would fail if no evidence were given.

The standard of proof in civil cases is the balance of probabilities - sometimes called proof on a preponderance of evidence. This means the party bearing the burden must satisfy the court that their version of events is more probable than not - more likely to be true than false. This is a lower standard than the criminal standard of beyond reasonable doubt.

Plaintiff Bears the Initial Burden

The plaintiff who brings a civil claim bears the initial burden of proving the elements of their cause of action. In a contract claim, the plaintiff must prove: a valid contract existed, the defendant breached it, and the plaintiff suffered loss as a result. Until the plaintiff adduces sufficient evidence on each of these elements, the defendant need not respond.

Once the plaintiff establishes a sufficient case on the balance of probabilities, the evidential burden effectively shifts to the defendant to adduce evidence in response. The defendant who adduces no response risks having judgment entered against them on the plaintiff's evidence alone.

Defendant's Burden on Affirmative Defences

Where the defendant raises an affirmative defence - a defence that goes beyond merely denying the plaintiff's case and asserts additional facts - the defendant bears the burden of proving that defence on the balance of probabilities.

In a land dispute in Uganda, the defendant who claims to have purchased the land from the original owner bears the burden of proving that purchase. In a contract case, the defendant who claims the contract was induced by fraud bears the burden of proving the fraud. In a negligence claim, the defendant who alleges contributory negligence bears the burden of proving it.

The Balance of Probabilities Standard

The balance of probabilities standard requires the court to determine which version of events is more probable. It does not require the party to eliminate all doubt - only to show that their version is more likely true than not, that the scales of probability tip in their favour even slightly.

Key Ugandan Civil Authority

Uganda Electricity Board v G.W. Musoke, Civil Appeal No. 30 of 1993 - The court examined the evidence in a civil matter and applied the balance of probabilities standard, illustrating how Ugandan civil courts weigh competing evidence to determine which party has discharged their burden.

Key Points - Page 5.2.3
  • Civil burden governed by Section 101 of the Evidence Act Cap. 8 - he who asserts must prove
  • Standard of proof in civil cases: balance of probabilities - more probable than not
  • Plaintiff bears initial burden on all elements of the cause of action
  • Defendant bears burden on affirmative defences raised
  • Both parties may bear burdens on different issues within the same proceedings
  • Balance of probabilities is lower than beyond reasonable doubt - the court weighs which version is more likely
  • Key authority: Uganda Electricity Board v G.W. Musoke, Civil Appeal No. 30 of 1993

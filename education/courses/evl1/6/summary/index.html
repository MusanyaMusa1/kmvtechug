Module 6 Summary

Review these key points before taking the Module 6 Graded Quiz.

  • The standard of proof is the degree of persuasion a court must reach before finding that a burden of proof has been discharged
  • Two standards apply in Uganda: beyond reasonable doubt (criminal) and balance of probabilities (civil)
  • The criminal standard is higher because wrongful conviction involves severe irreversible consequences - loss of liberty or life
  • Constitutional basis: Article 28(3)(a) of the Constitution of Uganda 1995 - presumption of innocence
  • Beyond reasonable doubt: the court must be sure - firmly convinced. Not absolute certainty, but significantly more than balance of probabilities
  • Reasonable doubt must be based on reason from the evidence - not speculation, sympathy, or imaginary doubt
  • Woolmington v DPP [1935] AC 462: the golden thread principle - prosecution bears burden of proving guilt throughout (persuasive)
  • Akbar Hussein Godi v Uganda CA No. 3 of 2013: suspicion however strong is not proof - convictions on suspicion are overturned (binding Ugandan authority)
  • Ntambala v Uganda CA No. 34 of 2015: the court must be sure, not merely satisfied on balance of probabilities (binding Ugandan authority)
  • Miller v Minister of Pensions [1947]: high degree of probability not certainty beyond shadow of a doubt (persuasive)
  • Balance of probabilities: more probable than not - the scales must tip in the party's favour even slightly
  • Uganda Electricity Board v G.W. Musoke CA No. 30 of 1993: illustrates the civil balance of probabilities standard in practice
  • Civil allegations of criminal conduct still use the civil standard - but more cogent evidence is required for serious allegations
  • A person acquitted criminally can be found civilly liable on the same facts - different standards produce potentially different outcomes
  • Defence of insanity: accused proves on balance of probabilities under Section 11, Trial on Indictments Act Cap. 25
  • Both standards can apply in one proceeding on different issues

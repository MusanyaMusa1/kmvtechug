Question 2 of 15
Which two standards of proof apply in Ugandan courts?
A
Prima facie evidence and conclusive proof
B

Uganda recognises two main standards: beyond reasonable doubt in criminal proceedings and balance of probabilities in civil proceedings.
Not correct. The two standards are beyond reasonable doubt (criminal) and balance of probabilities (civil). The correct answer is B.
Question 3 of 15
Why is the criminal standard of proof higher than the civil standard?
A
Because criminal courts have more judges
B
Because criminal cases involve more complex evidence
C
Because the consequences of a wrongful criminal conviction - loss of liberty or life - are more severe and irreversible than civil consequences
D
Because the Evidence Act Cap. 8 specifically requires a higher standard in criminal cases
The criminal standard is higher because wrongful criminal conviction involves severe irreversible consequences - loss of liberty or life. Wrongful civil judgments are generally more reversible. The law calibrates the standard to the severity of the consequences.
Not correct. The criminal standard is higher because wrongful conviction involves irreversible consequences - loss of liberty or life. The correct answer is C.
Question 4 of 15
Beyond reasonable doubt requires the court to be:
A
Absolutely certain with no doubt whatsoever
B
Satisfied that guilt is more likely than not
C
Sure - firmly convinced, not merely thinking guilt is more probable
D
Satisfied that the prosecution's case is stronger than the defence's case
Beyond reasonable doubt requires the court to be sure - firmly and confidently convinced of guilt. It does not require absolute mathematical certainty, but it requires significantly more than a balance of probabilities.
Not correct. Beyond reasonable doubt requires the court to be sure - firmly convinced. Not absolute certainty, but significantly more than balance of probabilities. The correct answer is C.

Section B - True or False (4 questions)
Question 9 of 15
A person acquitted of murder in a criminal trial cannot be found civilly liable for causing the same death.
False. An acquittal means the prosecution failed to reach beyond reasonable doubt. The civil standard is lower - balance of probabilities. A civil court can find liability on the same facts because the lower civil standard may be reached even when the higher criminal standard was not.
False is correct. An acquittal does not bar civil liability. Different standards apply and the lower civil standard may be met even where the criminal standard was not.
Question 10 of 15
The defence of insanity in a Ugandan criminal trial is proved by the accused on the balance of probabilities.
True. Under Section 11 of the Trial on Indictments Act Cap. 25, the accused bears the legal burden of proving insanity on the balance of probabilities. This is an exception to the general rule that the accused bears no legal burden.
True is correct. The defence of insanity places a legal burden on the accused to prove on the balance of probabilities.
Question 11 of 15
Reasonable doubt is any possible doubt that can be imagined about the accused's guilt.
False. Reasonable doubt is doubt based on reason arising from the evidence. It is not any possible or imaginary doubt. Speculative or fanciful theories of innocence do not create reasonable doubt. The doubt must be grounded in the actual evidence before the court.
False is correct. Reasonable doubt must be based on reason from the evidence - not speculation or imaginary doubt.
Question 12 of 15
The same facts can produce a criminal acquittal and a civil finding of liability because different standards of proof apply.
True. The criminal standard is beyond reasonable doubt. The civil standard is balance of probabilities. Facts insufficient to reach the criminal standard may well be sufficient to tip the civil balance. This explains why a person can be acquitted criminally but found civilly liable on the same events.
True is correct. Different standards explain why the same facts can produce different outcomes in criminal and civil proceedings.

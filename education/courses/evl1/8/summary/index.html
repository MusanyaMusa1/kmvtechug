Module 8 Summary

Review these key points from all three lessons before taking the Module 8 Graded Quiz.

1
Lesson 1: The Nature of Corroboration
  • Corroboration is independent evidence confirming a witness's testimony on a material particular and connecting the accused to the crime
  • R v Baskerville [1916] 2 KB 658: foundational definition - independent testimony affecting the accused by connecting him with the crime in a material particular (persuasive Commonwealth authority)
  • Three requirements: independent source, implicates the accused, goes to a material particular - ALL THREE must be satisfied
  • Can corroborate: physical evidence, independent testimony, electronic evidence, accused's deliberate lies about material matters
  • Cannot corroborate: complainant's own prior statements, one accomplice corroborating another, evidence only confirming a crime without implicating the accused
  • Modern Ugandan practice: moved from mandatory legal requirement to judicial warning approach in most categories
2
Lesson 2: Accomplice Evidence and Corroboration in Practice
  • Accomplice evidence is dangerous - accomplices may shift blame, lie for reduced sentences, or minimise their own role
  • Court must warn itself genuinely of the dangers before convicting on accomplice evidence
  • After a genuine warning, the court may convict on uncorroborated accomplice evidence if satisfied of reliability
  • One accomplice cannot corroborate another - both are in the same unreliable category
  • Ntambala v Uganda CA No. 34 of 2015: warnings must be genuine, not formulaic - courts must actually engage
  • Uganda v Ntambazi [1996] HCB 29: procedural safeguards for fair police identification parades - failure weakens evidence
  • Uganda v Doyi Wabwire Kyoyo (1976) HCB 212: procedural safeguards for magistrates taking confessions
3
Lesson 3: Sexual Offences, Children and Current Position
  • Historical mandatory corroboration warning in sexual offence cases: based on stereotypes - widely criticised and reformed
  • Modern position: mandatory warning modified - conviction possible on complainant's sole reliable testimony
  • Children's evidence: sworn where child understands oath, unsworn where child understands duty to tell truth
  • Modern position on children's evidence: court may act on reliable unsworn evidence after genuine assessment
  • Mandatory corroboration as absolute legal requirement most clearly survives for certain confession situations
  • Judicial warning approach applies for accomplice evidence, sexual offence complainants, and children's evidence
  • Children Act Cap. 62: protective provisions for child witnesses in Ugandan proceedings

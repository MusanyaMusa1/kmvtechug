Question 1 of 5
Article 28(3)(a) of the Constitution of Uganda 1995 elevates which presumption to constitutional status?
A
Presumption of death
B
Presumption of regularity
C
Presumption of innocence
D
Presumption of legitimacy
Article 28(3)(a) of the Constitution of Uganda 1995 guarantees the presumption of innocence - every person charged with a criminal offence shall be presumed innocent until proved guilty.
Not correct. Article 28(3)(a) guarantees the presumption of innocence. The correct answer is C.
Question 2 of 5
A child is born to a woman during her lawful marriage. What presumption applies to parentage?
A
The child is presumed illegitimate until the husband confirms paternity
B
The child is presumed to be the legitimate child of the husband - rebuttable by evidence of non-access during conception
C
No presumption - DNA evidence is required
D
The child is presumed legitimate and this is irrebuttable
The presumption of legitimacy applies - a child born during a lawful marriage is presumed to be the legitimate child of the husband. Rebuttable by evidence that the husband had no access to the wife during the period of conception.
Not correct. The presumption of legitimacy applies and is rebuttable by evidence of non-access. The correct answer is B.
Question 3 of 5
What primary fact triggers the presumption of death under Section 107 of the Evidence Act Cap. 8?
A
The person must have been declared missing by police
B
A death certificate must be produced
C
The person has not been heard of for seven years by those who would naturally have heard of them if alive
D
The person must have been absent from Uganda for ten years
Section 107 requires that the person has not been heard of for seven years by those who would naturally have heard of them if alive. The seven-year absence from relevant persons triggers the presumption.
Not correct. Section 107 requires seven years of absence from those who would naturally have heard. The correct answer is C.
Question 4 of 5
In Woolmington v DPP [1935] AC 462, Viscount Sankey described the presumption of innocence as:
A
The bedrock of all civil litigation
B
The golden thread running throughout criminal law
C
A procedural rule applying only at trial
D
A principle that can be overridden by strong circumstantial evidence
Viscount Sankey described the presumption of innocence as the golden thread running throughout criminal law - the principle that the prosecution must prove the prisoner's guilt throughout.
Not correct. Woolmington describes the presumption of innocence as the golden thread running throughout criminal law. The correct answer is B.
Question 5 of 5
Can the presumption of innocence be rebutted? If yes, how?
A
No - it is irrebuttable in all circumstances
B
Yes - by strong circumstantial evidence on the balance of probabilities
C
Yes - but only by proof beyond reasonable doubt by the prosecution
D
Yes - by the accused's failure to give evidence
The presumption of innocence is rebuttable - but only by the prosecution proving guilt beyond reasonable doubt. This is the highest standard and only this standard is sufficient to displace the constitutional presumption.
Not correct. The presumption of innocence is rebuttable but only by proof beyond reasonable doubt by the prosecution. The correct answer is C.

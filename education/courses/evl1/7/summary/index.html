Module 7 Summary

Review these key points from both lessons before taking the Module 7 Graded Quiz.

1
Lesson 1: Types of Presumptions
  • A presumption allows a court to take a fact as established once a primary fact is proved - without direct proof of the presumed fact
  • Two categories: rebuttable (displaced by contrary evidence) and irrebuttable (conclusive - cannot be challenged)
  • Presumptions directly interact with the burden of proof - they shift the evidential burden to the opposing party
  • Key rebuttable presumptions: death (Section 107 Evidence Act Cap. 8), legitimacy, regularity, sanity, continuance
  • Presumption of sanity: every person presumed sane - explains why accused must prove insanity on balance of probabilities
  • Most important irrebuttable presumption: child under twelve conclusively incapable of crime - Penal Code Act Cap. 128
  • Conclusiveness of judgment (res judicata) operates as an irrebuttable presumption
  • Registration of title under Registration of Titles Act Cap. 240 - conclusive presumption of ownership
2
Lesson 2: Presumptions in Practice
  • The presumption of innocence is the cornerstone of criminal justice - Article 28(3)(a) Constitution of Uganda 1995
  • Three consequences of the presumption of innocence: prosecution bears the burden; accused gets benefit of the doubt; beyond reasonable doubt standard applies
  • The presumption of innocence is rebuttable - but only by proof beyond reasonable doubt by the prosecution
  • Woolmington v DPP [1935] AC 462: the golden thread running throughout criminal law (persuasive)
  • Akbar Hussein Godi v Uganda CA No. 3 of 2013: treating failure to explain as guilt reverses the presumption (binding)
  • Ntambala v Uganda CA No. 34 of 2015: guilt must be proved not merely made probable (binding)
  • Section 107 Evidence Act Cap. 8: seven years absence without news creates rebuttable presumption of death
  • Section 108: no presumption of order of death where two persons die in a common disaster
  • Other key presumptions: books of account, old documents, legitimacy, regularity, electronic records

Rebuttable Presumptions

Video~12 minEvidence Act Cap. 8 - Section 107

PDF notes downloadable below.

Video Summary

This video examines rebuttable presumptions in depth - what makes a presumption rebuttable, how rebuttable presumptions shift the evidential burden, the five key rebuttable presumptions in Ugandan law including death (Section 107), legitimacy, regularity, sanity, and continuance, and how they arise in Ugandan family law, land law, and succession cases.

What Makes a Presumption Rebuttable?

A rebuttable presumption is one that the law allows but that the opposing party can challenge and displace by adducing sufficient contrary evidence. Once sufficient contrary evidence is adduced, the presumption is rebutted and ceases to apply. The court then decides the issue on all the evidence before it.

The word rebuttable is key - it means the presumption can be pushed back. It operates unless and until it is successfully challenged. The party against whom it operates bears the evidential burden of adducing sufficient evidence to rebut it.

How Rebuttable Presumptions Affect the Burden of Proof

When a rebuttable presumption operates, it effectively shifts the evidential burden to the opposing party. The party in whose favour the presumption operates does not need to prove the presumed fact - the law presumes it. The opposing party must then adduce evidence to rebut the presumption.

The standard required to rebut a presumption varies. Some can be displaced by any contrary evidence. Others require proof on the balance of probabilities. The specific standard depends on the particular presumption and its governing provision.

Key Rebuttable Presumptions in Ugandan Law

Five Key Rebuttable Presumptions

Presumption of death - Section 107, Evidence Act Cap. 8 - When a person has not been heard of for seven years by those who would naturally have heard of them if alive, the person is presumed dead. Rebuttable by evidence that the person is in fact alive.

Presumption of legitimacy - Children born during a lawful marriage are presumed to be the legitimate children of the husband. Rebuttable by evidence of non-access during the period of conception.

Presumption of regularity - Acts done in an official capacity are presumed to have been done properly and regularly (omnia praesumuntur rite esse acta). Rebuttable by evidence of irregularity or unauthorised conduct.

Presumption of sanity - Every person is presumed sane until the contrary is proved. This explains why the defence of insanity places the burden on the accused - they must rebut the presumption of sanity by proving insanity on the balance of probabilities under Section 11 of the Trial on Indictments Act Cap. 25.

Presumption of continuance - A state of affairs proved to exist at a particular time is presumed to continue until the contrary is shown.

Rebuttable Presumptions in Ugandan Practice

Rebuttable presumptions arise frequently in Ugandan family law, land law, and succession cases. The presumption of death is particularly important in succession cases where a missing person's estate must be administered. The presumption of legitimacy arises regularly in family law disputes about children's inheritance rights under the Succession Act Cap. 268.

In Ugandan land cases, the presumption of regularity is relevant where official land documents such as certificates of title are produced - they are presumed to be correctly issued until evidence of fraud or irregularity rebuts the presumption.

Key Points - Page 7.1.2
  • Rebuttable presumptions can be challenged and displaced by sufficient contrary evidence
  • They shift the evidential burden to the opposing party to adduce rebutting evidence
  • Key examples: presumption of death (Section 107 Evidence Act Cap. 8), legitimacy, regularity, sanity, continuance
  • Presumption of sanity explains why the accused bears the burden of proving insanity on the balance of probabilities
  • The standard to rebut varies by presumption - some require balance of probabilities, others any contrary evidence

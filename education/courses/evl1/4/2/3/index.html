Estoppel in Ugandan Courts

Text~9 min readingFinal page of Lesson 2

How Estoppel Operates in Ugandan Practice

Estoppel arises in Ugandan courts across a wide range of civil disputes. The most common contexts are land disputes, commercial contract matters, banking and finance cases, and employment disputes. Understanding how Ugandan courts approach estoppel arguments in practice is essential for anyone preparing to appear as an advocate.

Estoppel in Ugandan Land Disputes

Land disputes are the most common civil cases in Uganda at all levels from local council courts to the Supreme Court. Estoppel by conduct arises frequently because many Ugandan land arrangements are informal and long-standing conduct rather than formal written documents governs the relationship between parties.

Ugandan courts have consistently held that where a landowner's conduct over an extended period has represented that another person has rights in land, and that person has acted on that representation by developing the land and investing resources, the landowner may be estopped from denying those rights. Courts look carefully at the history of the relationship, the conduct of both parties, whether the alleged representations were sufficiently clear, and whether genuine detriment has been suffered.

Stanbic Bank Uganda Ltd v Uganda Crocs Ltd

Key Ugandan Appellate Authority

Stanbic Bank Uganda Ltd v Uganda Crocs Ltd, Civil Appeal No. 4 of 2004 - The Court of Appeal examined how representations made by a financial institution affected the rights and obligations of the other party. The case demonstrates the Ugandan appellate courts' careful approach to estoppel arguments - requiring clear establishment of all three elements before estoppel will be found to operate. A vague suggestion or general course of dealing that falls short of a clear representation will not suffice.

Estoppel and the Court Hierarchy

When raising estoppel arguments in Ugandan courts, advocates must be aware of the court hierarchy established by the Judicature Act Cap. 16. Estoppel by record created by a decision of the Supreme Court of Uganda binds all lower courts and all parties to those proceedings. Estoppel by record created by a decision of the Court of Appeal binds the High Court and lower courts. A decision of the High Court creates estoppel by record binding on the parties in all subordinate courts.

This hierarchy means that advocates checking estoppel by record arguments must always verify the level of the court that made the earlier decision.

Practical Advice for Raising Estoppel in Court

When raising estoppel as a defence in a Ugandan court, follow this approach.

First, identify the form of estoppel - is this estoppel by record, by deed, by conduct, or promissory estoppel? The form determines what must be proved and what authority to cite.

Second, establish each element clearly on the evidence. For estoppel by conduct under Section 114 - the representation, the reliance, and the detriment. Do not assume the court will infer elements not clearly established.

Third, cite the correct authority. Section 114 of the Evidence Act Cap. 8 for conduct estoppel. Stanbic Bank Uganda Ltd v Uganda Crocs Ltd as the key Ugandan appellate authority. Kimani v Gikanga (1965) EA 735 as persuasive East African authority. The High Trees case for promissory estoppel, clearly labelled as persuasive not binding.

Fourth, remember estoppel is a shield not a sword. Frame the argument as a defence against the other party's assertion, not as a freestanding cause of action.

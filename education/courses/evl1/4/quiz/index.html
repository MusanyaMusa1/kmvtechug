Section A - Multiple Choice (10 questions)
Question 1 of 20
Which section of the Evidence Act Cap. 8 is the primary provision governing estoppel by conduct?
A
Section 5
B
Section 30
C
Section 114
D
Section 65
Section 114 is the foundational estoppel provision in Uganda.
Not correct. Section 114 governs estoppel. The correct answer is C.
Question 2 of 20
A court has finally decided a company owes UGX 10 million to a supplier. The company brings fresh proceedings claiming it owes nothing. What prevents this?
A
Estoppel by deed
B
Estoppel by record - cause of action estoppel
C
Promissory estoppel
D
Estoppel by conduct
Estoppel by record - cause of action estoppel - prevents relitigating a cause of action already finally determined between the same parties.
Not correct. This is estoppel by record, cause of action estoppel. The correct answer is B.
Question 3 of 20
What are the three elements for estoppel under Section 114 of the Evidence Act Cap. 8?
A
Contract, consideration, and breach
B
Representation or conduct, reliance, and detriment
C
Writing, signature, and witnesses
D
Court order, execution, and service
The three elements are: a clear representation or conduct, actual reliance by the other party, and detriment suffered as a result.
Not correct. The three elements are representation, reliance, and detriment. The correct answer is B.
Question 4 of 20
Central London Property Trust Ltd v High Trees House Ltd [1947] KB 130 is authority for which doctrine?
A
Estoppel by record
B
Estoppel by deed
C
Promissory estoppel - preventing a party from resiling from a promise relied on to the promisee's detriment
D
The res judicata doctrine
The High Trees case is the foundation of promissory estoppel. It is cited as persuasive Commonwealth authority in Ugandan courts.
Not correct. High Trees is the foundation case for promissory estoppel. The correct answer is C.
Question 5 of 20
A seller executes a formal land transfer document stating the purchase price has been fully paid. He later claims payment was never made. What form of estoppel applies?
A
Promissory estoppel
B
Estoppel by record
C
Estoppel by deed
D
Estoppel by conduct
Estoppel by deed applies because the statement about payment was in a formal executed deed.
Not correct. This is estoppel by deed. The correct answer is C.
Question 6 of 20
Which correctly states the shield not sword principle?
A
Estoppel can only be raised in criminal proceedings
B
Estoppel can be used defensively to resist an inconsistent assertion but cannot be used as a standalone cause of action
C
Estoppel creates new rights that can be enforced independently
D
Estoppel can only be raised by the party who made the representation
The shield not sword principle means estoppel is used defensively - not as the basis of an independent claim.
Not correct. Estoppel is a shield - used defensively, not as a standalone cause of action. The correct answer is B.
Question 7 of 20
Promissory estoppel is suspensory rather than extinctive. This means:
A
It permanently destroys the promisor's legal rights
B
It applies only during court proceedings
C
It suspends the promisor's strict legal rights during the period of reliance but does not permanently extinguish them
D
It suspends court proceedings while negotiations continue
Promissory estoppel suspends rights during the period of reliance. Once circumstances change the promisor may be able to resume asserting their strict legal rights.
Not correct. Promissory estoppel suspends rights during reliance but does not permanently extinguish them. The correct answer is C.
Question 8 of 20
In Stanbic Bank Uganda Ltd v Uganda Crocs Ltd, Civil Appeal No. 4 of 2004, the Court of Appeal established which principle?
A
Promissory estoppel permanently extinguishes contractual rights
B
Estoppel requires clear establishment of all three elements and will not be applied loosely
C
Estoppel by deed applies to all written documents
D
Estoppel can override express statutory provisions
Stanbic Bank established that all three elements must be clearly proved before estoppel operates. Vague suggestions will not suffice.
Not correct. Stanbic Bank established that all three elements must be clearly proved. The correct answer is B.
Question 9 of 20
A landowner allows his neighbour to cultivate land for ten years and accepts payments described as appreciation. He now seeks to evict. Which form of estoppel is most relevant?
A
Estoppel by deed
B
Estoppel by record
C
Estoppel by conduct under Section 114
D
Promissory estoppel
Estoppel by conduct is most relevant. The long-standing conduct constitutes a representation of the neighbour's rights relied on to their detriment.
Not correct. This is estoppel by conduct under Section 114. The correct answer is C.
Question 10 of 20
Which of the following cannot be achieved through estoppel in Ugandan law?
A

B
Preventing a party from relitigating a matter already finally decided
C
Creating a new cause of action where no other legal basis exists
D
Preventing a seller from denying facts stated in a formal transfer deed
Estoppel cannot create a new cause of action where none otherwise exists - it is a shield not a sword.
Not correct. Estoppel cannot create a new cause of action. The correct answer is C.
Section B - True or False (5 questions)
Question 11 of 20
Estoppel by conduct can arise from silence where a party had a duty to speak and failed to correct a false impression.
True. Estoppel by conduct can arise from silence where there was a legal or moral duty to speak and correct a false impression.
True is correct. Silence where there was a duty to speak can create estoppel by conduct.
Question 12 of 20
Promissory estoppel can be used as the basis of an independent legal claim in Ugandan courts.
False. Promissory estoppel is a shield not a sword. It cannot be used as the basis of a standalone cause of action.
False is correct. Promissory estoppel is a shield not a sword.
Question 13 of 20
Issue estoppel prevents a party from relitigating a specific issue necessarily decided in earlier proceedings even if the cause of action in the second case is different.
True. Issue estoppel prevents specific issues already necessarily decided from being reopened in later proceedings between the same parties even in a different cause of action.
True is correct.
Question 14 of 20
Estoppel by conduct under Section 114 can override a clear express provision of an Act of Parliament.
False. Estoppel by conduct cannot override a clear statutory provision.
False is correct. Estoppel cannot override statute.
Question 15 of 20
Kimani v Gikanga (1965) EA 735 is binding authority in Ugandan courts on estoppel by conduct.
False. Kimani v Gikanga is a decision of the East African Court of Appeal and is persuasive authority in Ugandan courts - not binding.
False is correct. Kimani v Gikanga is persuasive, not binding, in Uganda.

Question 18 of 20 - Fill in the Blank

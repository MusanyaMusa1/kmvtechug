Module 4 Summary

Lesson 1: The Nature and Forms of Estoppel
  • Estoppel prevents a person from asserting or denying a fact inconsistent with a previous representation when another has relied on it to their detriment
  • Section 114 of the Evidence Act Cap. 8 is the foundational estoppel provision in Uganda
  • Three elements must all be present: a clear representation or conduct, actual reliance, and detriment suffered as a result
  • Estoppel is an evidence rule because it regulates what a party can assert before a court - it does not itself create or destroy substantive rights
  • Estoppel by record prevents relitigating matters already finally decided - operates in two forms: cause of action estoppel and issue estoppel
  • Four requirements for estoppel by record: competent court, final decision, same parties, same issue or cause of action
  • Only what was necessarily decided in the earlier judgment creates issue estoppel - not observations made in passing
  • Estoppel by deed prevents asserting facts inconsistent with a formal executed document
  • Estoppel by deed arises most commonly in Ugandan land transactions under the Registration of Titles Act Cap. 240 and the Land Act Cap. 236
2
Lesson 2: Estoppel by Conduct, Promissory Estoppel and Ugandan Practice
  • Estoppel by conduct is the broadest form - arising from acts, statements, or silence where there was a duty to speak
  • It arises most frequently in Ugandan land disputes where informal long-standing arrangements create representations
  • Key East African authority: Kimani v Gikanga (1965) EA 735 - persuasive (not binding) in Ugandan courts
  • Estoppel by conduct cannot override a clear express provision of statute
  • The shield not sword principle: estoppel is used defensively to prevent inconsistent assertions - it cannot create a new cause of action
  • Promissory estoppel prevents resiling from a clear promise about future conduct relied on to the promisee's detriment even without consideration
  • Foundation case: Central London Property Trust Ltd v High Trees House Ltd [1947] KB 130 (Lord Denning) - persuasive Commonwealth authority in Uganda
  • Promissory estoppel is suspensory not extinctive - suspends rights during the period of reliance, does not permanently extinguish them
  • Key Ugandan authority: Stanbic Bank Uganda Ltd v Uganda Crocs Ltd, CA No. 4 of 2004 - all three elements must be clearly proved
  • When raising estoppel in court: identify the form, prove each element, cite correct authority, frame as a defence

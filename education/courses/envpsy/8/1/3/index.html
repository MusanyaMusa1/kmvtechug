Poverty-Environment Nexus & the SDGs

Text~8 min readingSDGs

The poverty-environment cycle

Poverty drives environmental degradation when people lack access to sustainable alternatives - charcoal for cooking, wetland farming under food insecurity, informal settlement on protected land. Environmental degradation then deepens poverty by destroying the ecosystem services (food, water, livelihoods) that poor communities depend on. The cycle is self-reinforcing. Breaking it requires addressing poverty and environmental degradation simultaneously, not sequentially.

Sustainable Development Goals

The SDGs were adopted by UN member states in 2015. Goals most relevant to this course and to Uganda include: SDG 1 (No Poverty), SDG 6 (Clean Water and Sanitation), SDG 13 (Climate Action), SDG 14 (Life Below Water), and SDG 15 (Life on Land). SDG 15 is especially relevant given Uganda's exceptional biodiversity under threat.

Why psychology matters for the SDGs

Technical and economic solutions alone are not enough. Achieving the SDGs requires behaviour change, community participation, effective communication, and policies that account for values, norms, and barriers - the core of environmental psychology.

Key Points - Page 8.1.3
  • Poverty and environmental degradation reinforce each other.
  • Integrated approaches are required - not growth first, environment later.
  • SDGs 1, 6, 13, 14, and 15 are especially relevant to Uganda.
  • Environmental psychology is essential for achieving sustainable development.

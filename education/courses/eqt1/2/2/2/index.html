What is Rectification?

Rectification is the remedy that corrects a written document so that it reflects the true agreement of the parties. It is used when the written instrument, by mistake, fails to record what the parties actually agreed.

Common examples include land transfer forms, leases and written contracts that contain clerical or drafting errors.

The claimant must prove, to a high standard, that there was a prior common intention, that the intention continued up to the moment of execution, and that the written document fails to reflect that intention because of a mistake.

Important
Rectification is not available simply because a party made a bad bargain. It corrects the record of the agreement; it does not rewrite the bargain itself.

Key Points

  • Rectification aligns the written instrument with the true agreement.
  • High standard of proof of common intention and mistake is required.
  • It is not a tool for escaping a bad bargain.

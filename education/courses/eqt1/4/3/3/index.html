The Cy-près Doctrine

Video
Video uploading shortly. Read the notes below.

Video Summary

Cy-près allows the court to apply charitable property to a purpose as near as possible to the original intention when the original purpose fails. Initial failure usually requires a general charitable intention.

Meaning of Cy-près

Cy-près means “as near as possible”. When a charitable trust fails or becomes impossible or impractical to carry out, the court may apply the property to another charitable purpose that is as close as possible to the original intention.

Initial Failure

The charitable purpose is impossible from the outset. The court usually requires a general charitable intention before applying cy-près. If the settlor’s intention was limited to one specific purpose that has failed, the property may result to the settlor.

Subsequent Failure

The purpose was possible when the trust was created but later becomes impossible or impractical. Cy-près is more readily available because the property has already been dedicated to charity.

Key Points

  • Cy-près = as near as possible.
  • Initial failure – general charitable intention usually required.
  • Subsequent failure – cy-près more readily available.

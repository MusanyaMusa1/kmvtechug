Reception of Equity in Uganda

Video Summary

Equity was received into Uganda through the 1902 and 1911 Orders in Council. It came as a body of principles to be applied together with the common law. Today, section 14 of the Judicature Act Cap 16 requires courts to administer common law and Equity concurrently, with Equity prevailing in case of conflict. This is the modern statutory expression of the principle established in the Earl of Oxford's Case.

How Equity Came to Uganda

Uganda, like many Commonwealth countries, received English law through colonial legislation. The key instruments were the 1902 and 1911 Orders in Council. These Orders introduced English common law, the doctrines of Equity, and statutes of general application that were in force in England at the time.

Important point
Equity did not arrive in Uganda as a separate court system. It arrived as a body of principles that the courts were required to apply together with the common law.

Judicature Act Cap 16, Section 14

After independence this reception was continued and modernised by the Judicature Act. Under the current law the relevant provision is found in the Judicature Act Cap 16.

Section 14 of the Judicature Act Cap 16 is the most important section for this course. It requires the High Court and other courts to exercise their jurisdiction in conformity with the common law and the doctrines of Equity.

Specifically, section 14(2)(b) and section 14(4) provide that the courts shall administer common law and Equity concurrently, and that in case of conflict the rules of Equity shall prevail.

Key Points

  • Equity entered Uganda through the 1902 and 1911 Orders in Council.
  • It was received as principles, not as a separate court system.
  • Judicature Act Cap 16, section 14 is the key modern provision.
  • Courts must administer common law and Equity concurrently; Equity prevails in conflict.

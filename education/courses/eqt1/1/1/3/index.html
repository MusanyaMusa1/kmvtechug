Constitution Article 126(2)(e) and Key Ugandan Cases

Video10 minArt 126(2)(e)

Video uploading shortly. Read the notes below.

Video Summary

Article 126(2)(e) of the 1995 Constitution requires courts to administer justice without undue regard to technicalities. This constitutional provision strengthens the equitable principle of substance over form. Key Ugandan cases such as Stephen Mabosi v URA illustrate this approach. Together with the historical reception and the Judicature Act Cap 16, Article 126(2)(e) forms one of the three pillars of Equity in Uganda.

Constitutional Foundation

The 1995 Constitution of Uganda gives Equity a strong constitutional foundation. Article 126(2)(e) provides that in adjudicating cases of both a civil and criminal nature, the courts shall, subject to the law, apply the following principle: justice shall be administered without undue regard to technicalities.

This is sometimes called the principle of substance over form. It means that courts should focus on the real justice of the case rather than being trapped by pure technicalities.

Key Ugandan Cases

Stephen Mabosi v Uganda Revenue Authority
The court emphasised that Article 126(2)(e) requires justice to be administered without undue regard to technicalities.
Bank of Uganda v Hassan Bassajabalaba
A party who seeks Equity must do Equity. A person who has forged documents or acted with unclean hands cannot expect the court to grant equitable relief.

Key Points

  • Article 126(2)(e) requires justice without undue regard to technicalities.
  • This is the constitutional expression of substance over form.
  • Stephen Mabosi v URA is a leading illustration.
  • Equity in Uganda rests on three pillars: reception, Judicature Act Cap 16, and the Constitution.

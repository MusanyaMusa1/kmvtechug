Video Summary

The Maxims of Equity are short guiding principles that help courts apply equitable doctrines fairly. They are flexible tools of conscience rather than rigid rules. Ugandan courts regularly use them when granting equitable remedies or recognising equitable interests.

What are the Maxims?

The Maxims of Equity are short, memorable statements of principle. They guide judges when they exercise equitable jurisdiction. They are not rigid rules of law. They are flexible tools that help the court reach a just result according to conscience.

There is no fixed official list, but most textbooks and courts refer to a core group of about twelve well-known maxims. These maxims developed over centuries in the Court of Chancery and reflect the fundamental values of Equity: fairness, good conscience, and the prevention of unconscionable conduct.

Key Points

  • Maxims are guiding principles, not strict rules of law.
  • They reflect the values of fairness and good conscience.
  • Courts may apply more than one maxim in a single case.
  • They are frequently used by Ugandan judges.

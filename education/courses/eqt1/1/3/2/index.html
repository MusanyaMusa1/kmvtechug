Equity Prevails – Practical Ugandan Illustrations

Video
Video uploading shortly. Read the notes below.

Video Summary

In practice, Equity prevails when insistence on a strict legal right would be unconscionable. Ugandan courts have applied this in land and contract cases.

How the Principle Works

Suppose a person has a strict legal right under a written contract or registered title. At common law that right would normally succeed. However, if insistence on that right would be unconscionable (fraud, undue influence, or a clear agreement acted upon), Equity may intervene.

In land cases, Ugandan courts have recognised that an equitable interest can be enforced against a person with legal title, particularly where the legal owner is the original party or has knowledge of the earlier equitable interest.

Katarikawe v William Katwiremu
Often cited for the proposition that Equity acts in personam and can enforce equitable rights against the vendor of land.

Where parties have signed a land sale agreement and the purchaser has paid the price, courts have sometimes treated the purchaser as having an equitable interest even before formal transfer.

Key Points

  • Equity can override a strict legal right where reliance would be unconscionable.
  • Land sale cases frequently illustrate the principle.
  • The claimant must still meet the requirements of the remedy and have clean hands.

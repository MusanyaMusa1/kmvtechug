Complete Constitution

A trust is completely constituted when the trust property has been effectively vested in the trustee. There are two main methods: transfer of the property to the trustee in the manner appropriate to that type of property, or declaration by the settlor that he himself holds the property as trustee.

The Rule in Milroy v Lord

Equity will not perfect an imperfect gift. If a settlor intends to make a gift by transferring property to trustees but fails to complete the transfer, Equity will not treat the settlor as having declared himself trustee. The classic statement is found in Milroy v Lord (1862).

Key principle
There are limited exceptions, for example where the settlor has done everything in his power to transfer the property and the remaining steps are administrative. But the general rule remains strict: an incomplete transfer does not create a trust.

Key Points

  • Constitution requires vesting of the property in the trustee.
  • Transfer to trustee or declaration of self as trustee are the two methods.
  • Equity will not perfect an imperfect gift (Milroy v Lord).

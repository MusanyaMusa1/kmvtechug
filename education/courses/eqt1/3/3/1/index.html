Formalities – Especially Land under RTA Cap 240

Video
Video uploading shortly. Read the notes below.

Video Summary

Trusts of pure personalty generally need no writing. Trusts of land should be evidenced in writing and must comply with the formal requirements of the Registration of Titles Act Cap 240.

Personalty

For trusts of pure personalty (money, shares, chattels), Equity does not generally require writing. A trust can be declared orally, provided the three certainties are present.

Land

For trusts of land the position is stricter. Under the Registration of Titles Act Cap 240 and related land law, dispositions of interests in registered land generally require registration or at least a written instrument. A declaration of trust affecting land should be evidenced in writing to be enforceable.

Practical rule
Where the settlor declares himself trustee of land, writing is still required. Failure to comply does not always mean the trust is void for all purposes, but the safe practical rule is that trusts of land should be in writing.

Key Points

  • Personalty – writing not generally required.
  • Land – writing and registration requirements apply.
  • RTA Cap 240 is the key statute for registered land.

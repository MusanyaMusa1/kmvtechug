The Three Parties and Dual Ownership

Video
Video uploading shortly. Read the notes below.

Video Summary

A trust involves a settlor who creates it, a trustee who holds legal title, and a beneficiary who holds the equitable interest. The separation of legal and equitable ownership is the defining feature of the trust.

The Settlor

The settlor is the person who creates the trust by transferring property to the trustee or by declaring himself trustee. Once the trust is completely constituted, the settlor normally drops out of the picture unless he has reserved powers.

The Trustee

The trustee holds the legal title to the trust property. The trustee is under a fiduciary duty to manage the property for the benefit of the beneficiaries according to the terms of the trust and the general law.

The Beneficiary

The beneficiary holds the equitable or beneficial interest. Beneficiaries can enforce the trust against the trustee and, in many cases, against third parties who receive trust property with notice.

Dual ownership
The distinctive feature of the trust is the separation of legal and equitable title. The trustee has legal ownership; the beneficiary has equitable ownership.

Key Points

  • Settlor creates the trust.
  • Trustee holds legal title and is under fiduciary duties.
  • Beneficiary holds the equitable interest and can enforce the trust.
  • Dual ownership is the hallmark of the trust.

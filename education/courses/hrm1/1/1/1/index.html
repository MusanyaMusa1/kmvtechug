What Is HRM? Definition, Scope and the Employee Life Cycle

Text~8 min

Photo: Unsplash / Annie Spratt

Human Resource Management (HRM) is the strategic and coherent approach an organisation takes to managing its people. Its goal is to maximise employee performance and contribution to the organisation's success (Armstrong, 2006).

Employee life cycle

HRM covers the full relationship from attraction through exit: attraction, recruitment, onboarding, development, performance, compensation, and exit. In practice this means sourcing, developing, compensating, and maintaining/separating people.

Uganda example

At Stanbic Bank Uganda, HRM is the system that decides how staff are recruited, trained, paid, reviewed, and eventually exit - not merely "the people who hire you."

Key idea: HRM is strategic, not only administrative. Good HR decisions affect whether an organisation succeeds or struggles.

Key Points
  • HRM maximises contribution to organisational success
  • Employee life cycle: attraction to exit
  • HRM is strategic, not only admin

Idi Amin’s Grandson Disqualified in Commonwealth Games Boxing Quarter-Final

KM
King Musah Media News Desk
Published 30 July 2026 · English
📷 amin's son in the ring

Glasgow, Scotland — Ugandan super-heavyweight boxer Aziz Abdul, grandson of former military ruler Idi Amin, saw his medal ambitions end dramatically at the 2026 Commonwealth Games after he was disqualified for headbutting England’s Damar Thomas in their men’s 90+kg quarter-final.

Abdul, who fights under the nickname “Ringo,” received three warnings before referee intervention early in the third round on Wednesday. Trailing on the scorecards at the time of the stoppage, the 25-year-old Ugandan expressed strong dissatisfaction with the decision, suggesting bias and claiming an elbow from Thomas contributed to the head contact. Thomas advances to the semi-finals with at least a bronze medal guaranteed.

The bout had drawn significant attention due to Abdul’s family lineage. Idi Amin, who ruled Uganda from 1971 to 1979, was himself a noted boxer in his younger years, holding the national light-heavyweight title for nearly a decade. Abdul had spoken ambitiously ahead of the contest, expressing a desire to win gold in Glasgow — a city linked to his grandfather’s self-proclaimed “King of Scotland” title in the 1970s — and bring Uganda its first Commonwealth boxing medal since 1990.

A Story Beyond the Ring

Abdul had never met his grandfather, who died in exile in Saudi Arabia in 2003 when Abdul was a toddler. While acknowledging the complex legacy, the boxer has drawn inspiration from Amin’s sporting past rather than his controversial political rule, which is widely condemned for widespread human rights abuses.In the build-up, Abdul arrived in Scotland enthusiastic, even raising the Scottish flag and promising his powerful “Ringo punch” would deliver victory. His disqualification ends Uganda’s immediate medal hopes in boxing at these Games but highlights the growing profile of Ugandan talent on the international stage.Thomas, the home fighter, showed composure and effective boxing before the stoppage. He now eyes a place in the final as the tournament progresses at the Scottish Exhibition Centre.Uganda Boxing Federation officials and fans back home will no doubt review the officiating closely, while Abdul returns to Kampala with valuable experience from his debut Commonwealth Games appearance. The incident serves as a reminder of the fine margins and intense scrutiny in elite amateur boxing.King Musah Media will keep tracking African performances at the Glasgow 2026 Commonwealth Games.

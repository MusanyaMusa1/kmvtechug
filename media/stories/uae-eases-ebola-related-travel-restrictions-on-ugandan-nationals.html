UAE Eases Ebola-Related Travel Restrictions on Ugandan Nationals

KM
King Musah Media News Desk
Published 28 September 2026 · English
📷 A official announcement document detailing UAE travel regulations for international visitors

The United Arab Emirates (UAE) has officially eased entry and visa restrictions previously imposed on Ugandan nationals following the successful containment of the Ebola virus outbreak in Uganda.

The decision was jointly announced by the UAE's National Emergency Crisis and Disaster Management Authority, in coordination with the Ministry of Health and Prevention and the Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Port Security. The precautionary measures were first introduced on June 6, 2026, suspending new visas to prevent the importation of the disease.

Epidemiological clearance. UAE authorities relaxed the rules after Uganda completed the required international monitoring period without recording any new confirmed cases. However, similar entry restrictions remain active for citizens of the Democratic Republic of Congo and South Sudan due to ongoing health risks in those countries.

Economic relief and diplomatic impact. Welcoming the decision, Uganda’s Foreign Affairs official Adonia Ayebare described the move as a crucial step toward restoring full economic and diplomatic ties between Kampala and the Gulf state. While the resumption of visa issuances brings relief to thousands of Ugandan traders, students, and labor migrants, business leaders have noted that the temporary ban highlighted the vulnerability of regional trade to sudden health-related border closures.

King Musah Media will continue to follow developments regarding international travel regulations and diplomatic ties affecting Ugandans abroad.

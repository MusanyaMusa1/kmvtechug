Busoga University Reopening Delayed as Government Awaits Museveni’s Verdict on Name

KM
King Musah Media News Desk
Published 30 July 2026 · English
📷 Busoga University Building

Iganga, Uganda — Hopes for Busoga University to welcome students for the August 2026 intake have been put on hold. The Ugandan government is waiting for President Yoweri Museveni’s decision on the institution’s official name before completing the legal processes to establish it as a public university.

Physical infrastructure at the Iganga campus is ready after a major government-funded refurbishment valued at Shs 3.795 billion, executed by the UPDF Civil Engineers Brigade and handed over on July 20, 2026. The National Council for Higher Education (NCHE) has completed its inspection, yet bureaucratic steps — including the formation of a university council, appointment of management, and staff recruitment — remain pending.Acting Minister of Education and Sports, Dr John Chrysostom Muyingo, explained that stakeholder consultations on possible names, such as retaining “Busoga University” or adopting alternatives like “Kiira University,” have largely concluded. “We have now concluded the consultations and are left with only one stakeholder, the President,” he said. A transition task force has submitted its report seeking Museveni’s views. Once approved, the proposal will proceed to Cabinet and Parliament for a statutory instrument.

From Private Troubles to Public Ambition

Busoga University, founded in 1999 and affiliated with the Busoga Diocese of the Church of Uganda, faced significant challenges. It was closed in 2017 by the NCHE following investigations that revealed unqualified academic staff, unaccredited programmes, and the fraudulent award of degrees to more than 1,000 students, including many from South Sudan.In January 2018, President Museveni directed the Ministry of Education to take over both Busoga University and Mountains of the Moon University as public institutions. The Church of Uganda has supported the transition and retains ownership of over 200 acres of land at the site.Local leaders and church officials have welcomed the developments while urging speed. Iganga South MP Andrew Kaluya Namitego described the project as a long-held regional dream. “We are very happy that Busoga University is finally taking shape... We are fast-tracking the issue of the statutory instruments,” he noted.Rt Rev Patrick Wakula, Bishop of Busoga Central Diocese, expressed optimism: “It is my prayer that we begin as early as possible. We have gone through a lot of history, but even those institutions that have reached where they are today began with one step.” Prof John Tabuti, chairperson of the transition task force, appealed for continued stakeholder support.

Implications for Students and the Region

The delay means government-sponsored students for the new academic year have already been allocated to other public universities. Private admissions continue elsewhere, potentially affecting prospective students from Busoga and surrounding areas who were counting on local access to higher education.Critics may see the situation as emblematic of decision-making processes in Uganda that concentrate significant authority at the highest level. Supporters, however, point to the substantial public investment and the potential for a revitalised institution to drive regional development, create jobs, and expand access to quality tertiary education.Stakeholders from the Church, local MPs, and the task force remain hopeful that the remaining processes will be completed swiftly once the President’s input is received, allowing the university to open its doors in the near future.King Musah Media will continue to monitor developments as this important regional higher education project moves forward.

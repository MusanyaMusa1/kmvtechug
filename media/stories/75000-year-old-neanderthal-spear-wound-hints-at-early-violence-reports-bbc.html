📷 75,000-Year-Old Neanderthal Spear Wound Hints at Early Violence, Reports BBC

According to reporting by BBC News, new archaeological insights suggest that lethal violence may have played a defining role in human history much earlier than scientists previously believed.

The findings center on a 75,000-year-old Neanderthal spear wound, which offers rare physical evidence of lethal fighting among early hominins. Researchers note that this discovery challenges existing timelines regarding when organized or fatal conflict became common.

Readers interested in the complete details of the discovery can find the full original report at BBC News.

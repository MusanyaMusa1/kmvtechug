Police Urge Peace and Order as Uganda Prepares for Local Council Elections Today

KM
King Musah Media News Desk
Published 27 July 2026 · English
📷 Police Urguing Ugandans On Elections

With campaigns closed yesterday (Monday, July 27, 2026) and voting scheduled for Today, July 28, 2026, the Uganda Police Force has issued a strong public reminder to candidates, their supporters, and the general public to uphold peace, lawfulness, and order throughout the Local Council (LC) elections.

In a statement shared on their official X account, police spokesperson and other security stakeholders emphasized zero tolerance for any form of misconduct that could undermine the integrity of the electoral process. The message comes at a critical moment when grassroots leadership positions — vital for local governance, dispute resolution, and community development — are up for grabs across the country.Context of the Local Council ElectionsLocal Council elections in Uganda represent one of the most decentralized and community-driven aspects of the country’s democratic system. LC positions, from village (LC1) to higher local levels, play key roles in everyday governance: mobilizing communities for development projects, maintaining local security, resolving minor disputes, and linking citizens with higher government structures.These elections often see high participation because they directly affect people’s daily lives. However, they can also be intense due to personal rivalries, clan or religious influences, resource competition, and occasional political alignments with national parties. Past cycles have occasionally witnessed voter bribery, intimidation, vote rigging allegations, violence, and disputes over voter registers or candidate eligibility.Police Message: A Call to ResponsibilityThe police statement, attributed in part to @mkituuma

, stresses that:The electoral process must remain peaceful, lawful, and orderly.

All forms of misconduct — including violence, hate speech, voter intimidation, destruction of campaign materials, or any acts that disrupt voting — will not be tolerated.

Security and administrative teams are deployed and vigilant to ensure smooth proceedings.

This proactive communication aligns with standard pre-election security protocols. It serves both as a deterrent and a reassurance to voters that authorities are monitoring the situation closely.Broader Picture: Opportunities and ChallengesPositive Aspects:Grassroots elections strengthen local democracy and give ordinary citizens a direct say in who leads their villages and parishes.

High turnout can enhance accountability, as local leaders are often more accessible than national politicians.

Peaceful polls would reflect maturing democratic norms at the community level.

Potential Risks:In some areas, competition can turn personal or ethnic, leading to tensions.

Logistical challenges such as inadequate voting materials, voter register inaccuracies, or accessibility issues in remote regions sometimes arise.

Economic pressures may tempt candidates or agents into vote-buying or other malpractices.

Misinformation on social media or through word-of-mouth can inflame unnecessary conflicts.

Security forces, the Electoral Commission, and civil society organizations typically collaborate during such exercises to address these issues in real time.A Balanced Call to ActionFor a successful election, responsibility lies with multiple stakeholders:Candidates: Campaign on issues (service delivery, local development, integrity) rather than personal attacks. Concede gracefully where defeated and pursue disputes through legal channels.

Supporters and Voters: Exercise democratic rights responsibly — vote peacefully, respect differing choices, and avoid provocation.

Security Agencies: Enforce the law impartially, without bias toward any side.

Electoral Commission: Ensure transparency, timely resolution of complaints, and credible results.

Media and Civil Society: Report factually and promote peace messaging.

Uganda has made notable progress in managing elections with reduced large-scale violence in recent cycles, though isolated incidents remain a concern. Tomorrow’s polls offer another opportunity to demonstrate maturity in local democratic practice.As voting begins, King Musah Media urges all Ugandans to prioritize peace and national unity. Let the voice of the people prevail in an atmosphere of calm and fairness. The eyes of the nation and the world will be on how communities conduct themselves during this important grassroots democratic exercise.Stay safe, vote wisely, and may the best leaders emerge for the service of their communities.

We will monitor developments and provide updates on the conduct and outcomes of the July 28, 2026 Local Council elections.

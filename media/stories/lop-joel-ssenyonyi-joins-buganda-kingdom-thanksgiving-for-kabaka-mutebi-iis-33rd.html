LoP Joel Ssenyonyi Joins Buganda Kingdom Thanksgiving for Kabaka Mutebi II’s 33rd Coronation Anniversary

KM
King Musah Media News Desk
Published 31 July 2026 · English
📷 LoP at the 33rd Kabaka Coronation

Bweyogerere, Uganda — Leader of Opposition Joel Ssenyonyi on Friday attended a special thanksgiving service at Watoto Church in Bweyogerere to mark the 33rd anniversary of the coronation of Kabaka Ronald Muwenda Mutebi II of Buganda. Ssenyonyi joined other political, cultural, and religious leaders in honouring the Kabaka’s three decades on the throne, a milestone symbolising unity, cultural resilience, and development within the kingdom.

Dressed formally, the LoP was seen engaging warmly with fellow attendees during the celebrations, reflecting the cross-aisle respect often extended to traditional institutions in Uganda’s political landscape.

Cross-Partisan Recognition of Cultural Heritage

The #Amatikkira33 events have drawn leaders from across the political divide, underscoring the unifying role of the Buganda Kingdom. Kabaka Mutebi II ascended the throne on July 31, 1993, and has since championed initiatives in education, healthcare, agriculture, and youth empowerment that benefit millions.Prime Minister Robinah Nabbanja earlier represented the government at the same venue, highlighting cordial relations between the central government and the kingdom.

Broader Significance

Participation by opposition figures like Ssenyonyi demonstrates that cultural milestones often transcend partisan lines. The Kabaka’s reign has focused on restoring Buganda’s pride and advancing practical development programmes that complement national efforts.As celebrations continue across Buganda, the anniversary serves as a reminder of the important place traditional leaders occupy in Uganda’s social fabric. Subjects and observers have used the occasion to reaffirm loyalty to the Kabaka while praying for continued peace and progress.King Musah Media congratulates His Majesty Kabaka Ronald Muwenda Mutebi II on this significant milestone. Long live the King!

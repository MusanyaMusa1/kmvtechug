Masaka Road Horror: Reckless Driving Claims More Lives as Uganda’s Killer Highways Claim Another Tragedy

MM
Musanya Musa
Published 26 July 2026 · English
📷 Road Accident in Masaka

A devastating road crash on the Masaka Road has once again exposed the deadly state of Uganda’s highways. The accident, which occurred just hours ago, has resulted in multiple fatalities and left several others injured. Among the survivors is popular Sheikh Shafik Mafo, whose narrow escape has drawn widespread attention and relief.

This latest incident adds to the grim tally on one of Uganda’s most dangerous routes. Speeding, dangerous overtaking, driver fatigue, and poorly maintained vehicles remain the primary culprits, according to eyewitness accounts and recurring patterns.Why Masaka Road Keeps Claiming LivesHigh traffic volume combined with weak enforcement.

PSV operators racing for trips and profits.

Inadequate road safety infrastructure and signage.

Culture of impunity among some drivers and fleet owners.

The fact that a prominent religious leader like Sheikh Shafik Mafo was involved only highlights what ordinary Ugandans face daily. If high-profile individuals are not spared, the vulnerability of the average commuter is even greater.Unapologetic Truth: Uganda is in a road safety crisis. Every week, families lose loved ones not because of “accidents,” but because of systemic negligence — poor regulation, corruption in vehicle inspection, and failure to punish reckless behavior. The Masaka Road has become a symbol of this national shame. Enough condolences. We need enforcement, accountability, and infrastructure that saves lives.

The Way ForwardImmediate deployment of speed cameras and stricter traffic patrols on high-risk roads.

Heavy fines, license suspensions, and vehicle impoundment for violators.

Mandatory fitness checks for all PSVs and prosecution of fleet owners who enable dangerous practices.

Public campaign to shame and report reckless driving.

The Uganda Police, UNRA, and Ministry of Works must treat road safety as a national emergency, not a seasonal press release issue. Sheikh Shafik Mafo and other survivors need full support and recovery. The families of the deceased deserve more than thoughts and prayers they deserve justice and concrete reforms so no more blood is spilled on Masaka Road.This tragedy is preventable. The question is whether those in authority have the political will to act before the next crash makes headlines.

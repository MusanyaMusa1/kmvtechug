King Oyo: The Boy King, the Succession War and a Kingdom Left in Uncertainty

KM
King Musah Media News Desk
Published 13 September 2026 · English
📷 The late King Oyo’s casket during the final journey to Karambi Royal Tombs on 12 September 2026

On Saturday, 12 September 2026, the late Omukama Oyo Nyimba Kabamba Iguru Rukidi IV was laid to rest at Karambi Royal Tombs in Fort Portal. The date carried a powerful historical weight. It was exactly 31 years to the day since a three-year-old prince was installed as King of Tooro on 12 September 1995, becoming one of the world’s youngest reigning monarchs.

What should have been a moment of pure national mourning instead became the climax of a deeply contested transition that exposed divisions within the royal family, the Babiito clan and the wider kingdom.

The Death and the Return Home

King Oyo died on 27 August 2026 in Dallas, Texas, at the age of 34. He had been battling angiosarcoma, a rare and aggressive form of cancer. According to details later revealed by Princess Ruth Nsemere Komuntale and representatives of Queen Mother Best Kemigisa, the King had spent more than a year seeking treatment for unexplained pain. Doctors in Uganda initially linked the discomfort to exercise or ulcers.

A correct diagnosis only came in mid-October 2025 after advanced tests in Nairobi. He later received chemotherapy in Germany before being moved to the United States. For a period the treatment appeared to work and he regained strength, but his condition deteriorated sharply in July 2026. He remained communicative until the final day, when he was placed on life support and pronounced dead at 2pm US time.

His body arrived at Entebbe International Airport on 4 September. President Yoweri Museveni received the casket, laid a wreath and directed that the late monarch be accorded full state burial honours. The remains were later transferred to Karuziika Palace in Fort Portal, where they lay in state during the traditional nine-day mourning period.

Nine Days of Mourning and National Presence

During the mourning period, Karuziika Palace became the centre of national attention. Daily prayers were held. Thousands of ordinary Batooro, cultural leaders and government officials filed past the casket.

Buganda Kingdom showed strong solidarity. The Nnabagereka, Sylvia Nagginda, spent several hours at the palace and later attended the funeral service. Katikkiro Charles Peter Mayiga was also present at the cathedral service, alongside leaders from Bunyoro, Busoga and other cultural institutions. Their presence underscored the respect King Oyo commanded beyond Tooro.

The Succession Battle

The most explosive element of the entire period was the fight over who should succeed him.

The late King left a will dated 14 September 2022. In it, he directed that if he was survived by a lawfully recognised biological son, that son should inherit the throne. The identity of the child has never been publicly disclosed, and the Babiito clan has disputed whether such a son exists or meets traditional requirements.

On 9 September, a 21-member Royal Succession Committee of the Babiito clan announced Prince Edward Rukidi Kijanangoma, a news anchor and editor at Uganda Broadcasting Corporation and a cousin of the late King, as the new Omukama. Earlier, names such as George Desmond Kamurasi had also circulated, but he reportedly declined.

Queen Mother Best Kemigisa and Princess Ruth Komuntale immediately rejected the decision. They insisted the will must be respected and accused the committee of acting while the King’s body was still lying in state. The Queen Mother also claimed the royal family was under effective house arrest after traditional guards were removed and security forces deployed around the palace. Government later dismissed the house-arrest claims, saying the deployment was for security.

The tension between the Queen Mother and Omusuuga Charles Akiiki Kamurasi, head of the Babiito clan, became one of the most visible fault lines in the crisis.

The Burial and the Rituals

The funeral service was held at St John’s Cathedral in Fort Portal, the same church where the late King was baptised as a baby. Archbishop Stephen Kazimba Mugalu led the service. After the service, the casket was taken on a roughly six-kilometre procession to Karambi Royal Tombs.

Access to the actual burial ground was restricted. Traditional rites were performed, including the key succession ritual in which the incoming king casts nine coffee berries into the grave. Reports indicate that Edward Rukidi Kijanangoma performed this rite, marking the formal traditional handover according to the Babiito process.

There were, however, reports of tension at the tombs. Claims circulated that the Queen Mother was restricted or locked in a vehicle and that the handling of the casket was forceful. While the burial ultimately took place, these accounts reflected the depth of the unresolved conflict even at the final moment.

Money, Traders and the Cost of Farewell

The official event-management budget for the funeral and burial was estimated at approximately Shs 1.72 billion before VAT, rising to around Shs 2 billion when tax is included. The government supported significant parts of the costs, including medical and repatriation expenses, food for mourners and aspects of the funeral arrangements.

Around Karuziika Palace and Fort Portal, local traders, food vendors, transporters and informal businesses recorded a sharp increase in activity as thousands of mourners converged on the city.

What Remains Unanswered

Several critical questions remain open:

The identity and status of the biological son named in the late King’s will.

Whether the selection of Edward Rukidi Kijanangoma will hold or face further legal and cultural challenge.

The future role and residence of Queen Mother Best Kemigisa. Karuziika Palace has historical links to support once provided by the late Libyan leader Muammar Gaddafi, and the Queen Mother has long been associated with the palace.

How the kingdom will heal the divisions exposed during the succession fight.

What becomes of the considerable royal estate that accompanies the throne.

A Kingdom at a Crossroads

King Oyo’s reign began in childhood and ended in controversy. He grew up in the public eye, modernised aspects of the kingdom while trying to preserve tradition, and left behind a complex legacy.

His burial on the same date he was crowned closed one chapter. The succession battle, the silence around the claimed heir, the unresolved tensions within the royal family and the questions hanging over the palace and the new leadership mean that another chapter has only just begun.

Tooro now faces the difficult task of moving from mourning a king who was once a boy into deciding what kind of kingdom it will be under the next Omukama.

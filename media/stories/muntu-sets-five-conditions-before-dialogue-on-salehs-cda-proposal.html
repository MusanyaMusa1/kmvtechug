Muntu Sets Five Conditions Before Dialogue on Saleh’s CDA Proposal

KM
King Musah Media News Desk
Published 25 November 2026 · English
📷 Maj Gen (Rtd) Mugisha Muntu speaking on conditions for national dialogue

Alliance for National Transformation (ANT) president Maj Gen (Rtd) Mugisha Muntu has outlined five confidence-building measures that the government must fulfil before any meaningful dialogue on Gen Salim Saleh’s proposed Consociational Democracy Agreement (CDA) can take place.

Muntu said ANT remains open to dialogue but will not be compromised, bought or dragged into a process that does not put Uganda first.The five measures, which he said were jointly agreed with the National Unity Platform (NUP) and the People’s Front for Freedom (PFF), include: :

The unconditional release of political prisoners and accounting for missing persons, including the granting of mandatory bail to those held beyond constitutional limits.

Creating conditions that allow Ugandans forced into exile to return safely.

An end to alleged abductions, torture, unlawful detention and state-sponsored intimidation based on political beliefs.

The reopening of political and civic space so that parties, civil society organisations and non-governmental groups can operate freely within the law.

Ensuring the political neutrality of security agencies, including the withdrawal of uniformed military officers from partisan political activities.

Muntu questioned how opposition leaders can be expected to engage in talks while people continue to be picked up from the streets and media houses. He specifically pointed to the prolonged detention of opposition figure Dr Kizza Besigye and others who have spent more than 400 days in custody despite constitutional provisions on mandatory bail.“We are presenting five specific issues to the regime as confidence-building measures so that we detect whether there is goodwill on the side of those in government,” Muntu said.Despite being locked out of the Interparty Organisation for Dialogue (IPOD), ANT has applied for membership after securing representation in Parliament, though admission is yet to be formalised.The remarks come amid renewed discussion around Gen Saleh’s CDA proposal, which seeks to address root causes of sectarianism, exclusion and political conflict through a form of power-sharing arrangement. Some IPOD parties have shown willingness to integrate the idea into the broader national dialogue framework, while other opposition groups have set their own terms.

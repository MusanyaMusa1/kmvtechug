Veteran Playwright and Actor Alex Mukulu Dies at 72

KM
King Musah Media News Desk
Published 15 August 2026 · English
📷 Veteran playwright and actor Alex Mukulu

Uganda’s arts community is mourning the death of veteran playwright, actor, musician and theatre director Alex Mukulu, who has passed away at the age of 72. The news, first reported by several media houses and confirmed across entertainment platforms on Saturday, 15 August 2026, has triggered an outpouring of tributes from artists, journalists and cultural enthusiasts. Born in 1954 in Misebe, Ssingo County in Buganda, Mukulu studied Film and Drama at Makerere University after attending Kololo Secondary School. He went on to become one of the most influential figures in Ugandan theatre over more than four decades. His best-known works include the plays “30 Years of Bananas,” “Wounds of Africa,” “Guest of Honour,” “Excuse Me Mzungu,” and “Journey to Self-Realization,” the latter performed at the opening of the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting in Kampala in 2007. He was also a musician and composer whose work cut across political, social and cultural themes. Mukulu was widely respected for using theatre as a tool to mirror society, challenge power and reflect the lived experiences of ordinary Ugandans. In recent years he returned to the stage with productions that continued to spark conversation. Tributes have described him as a forefather of modern Ugandan theatre whose contribution paved the way for younger generations of actors, playwrights and performers. Many have hailed his creativity, courage and lasting impact on the country’s cultural landscape. Details regarding the cause of death and funeral arrangements had not been officially released by the time of this report. The loss of Alex Mukulu marks the end of an era in Ugandan performing arts. His body of work remains a significant part of the nation’s cultural heritage. King Musah Media joins the creative fraternity in mourning the veteran artiste and extends condolences to his family, friends and colleagues

JUDICIARY TIGHTENS MEDIA ACCESS RULES FOR BESIGYE TREASON CASE

KM
King Musah Media News Desk
Published 29 July 2026 · English
📷 Kizza Byesigye in court

The Judiciary of Uganda has issued revised guidelines for the high profile treason trial of retired Colonel Kizza Besigye and two others. The new rules focus on security screening and access controls as the case resumes amid heightened public interest.DETAILS OF REVISED GUIDELINESUnder the updated measures all media equipment including cameras audio recorders laptops and other electronic devices must undergo mandatory security screening before entry into the court premises. Requests for live broadcasts of proceedings require prior submission to the Public Relations Office for approval. These steps come as the trial faces continued scrutiny over procedural matters and public access.CONTEXT OF THE ONGOING TRIALThe guidelines were announced as Besigye appeared in court where he later collapsed leading to suspension of proceedings. The accused has objected to proceeding without his chosen lawyers citing the arrest of his lead counsel and the denial of entry to Kenyan lawyer Martha Karua. The case which has run since last year without bail granted to Besigye continues to draw political attention.REACTIONS AND CONCERNSSupporters of Besigye and some legal observers argue that the restrictions could limit transparency and the constitutional right to a public trial. Others including judicial officials maintain that the measures are necessary for security order and protection of witnesses in a sensitive matter.President Museveni has previously stated that delays in the trial stem from actions by the accused rather than the courts. The opposition views the process as politically driven.IMPLICATIONS FOR PUBLIC INTERESTThe tightened rules have sparked debate on balancing judicial security needs with the principles of open justice in Uganda. As Besigye receives medical care following his court collapse questions persist about the trial timeline and the health of the accused.King Musah Media remains committed to providing factual and balanced coverage of this developing story.
